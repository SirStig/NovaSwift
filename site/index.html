The 2002 space classic, flying again on modern Apple devices.

EV Nova (Ambrosia Software, 2002) is one of the deepest space trading-and-combat games ever made. Today it won't launch on a modern Mac, and it never came to mobile. NOVA Swift rebuilds it from scratch in Swift. Point it at a copy you already own and it runs natively — Mac, iPad, iPhone, and Apple TV.

What you can do today

It's 1177. You have a Shuttle and no plans.

The Federation is rotting from the inside, the Auroran Empire is tearing itself apart over honor, and the Polaris won't say what they know. This is all of EV Nova — and the clock keeps ticking whether you're paying attention or not.

Fly & fight

The momentum-heavy flight the original was built on — you don't turn, you swing the nose around and keep going the way you were. Lock a target, strip its shields, watch the ion cannons leave it drifting.

Explore & trade

Hyperjump between hundreds of systems on real fuel and work the spread: buy food where it's cheap, sell it where the war made it dear. Then trade the Shuttle up to something with real guns bolted on.

Play the story

Pick a scenario and take the missions people offer you. Fly for the Federation or defect to the Rebellion, get pulled into the Auroran succession, find out what the Vell-os are. Campaigns branch; the news reacts.

Fight dirty — and lose for real

Board a disabled freighter for its cargo, or fly the hull home with a prize crew. Demand tribute from a planet and it pays you daily. Shoot the wrong ship and the government remembers — and when your armor hits zero you punch out in an escape pod, if you bought one.

Meet the locals

Named captains with their own lines turn up in the shipping lanes, hired escorts draw a wage whether they're useful or not, and there's always the holovid races to throw credits at.

Play together

The one thing the 2002 original never had. Your friend keeps their own galaxy and pilot, but in a shared system you fly it together — same NPCs, same fight, real damage. PvP stakes are yours to set, from safe sparring to real death.

Couch play, on the big screen

Full gamepad support — twin-stick flight, every button remappable — on Mac, iPad and iPhone, and it's how you play on Apple TV: pair a controller and fly from the couch, with a 10-foot UI built for the distance.

New on top of the old

Modern touches — classic at heart.

One rule: anything that wasn't in the 2002 original can be switched off. Play it pure and it behaves like the game you remember.

Touch controls that feel right

Built for a screen you hold, not a desktop UI squeezed onto glass. Keyboard and mouse still work great on the Mac.

A live story map

Every campaign in your data, pannable and zoomable, drawn against how far your pilot has actually gotten.

Plug-ins without leaving the game

Browse and install community plug-ins and total conversions from inside the app.

Classic / Enhanced toggles

Keep everything original, or opt into the modern layer piece by piece. Nothing is ever forced on you.

Import once, play everywhere

Import your data once and it syncs through your own private iCloud, so your other devices — a freshly set-up Apple TV included — pick it up automatically. Nothing leaves your account.

Where we're at — honestly

The game is here. Now we're sharpening it.

You can play the whole of EV Nova today, start to finish, on all four platforms. Flight and combat, the economy, branching campaigns, boarding and capture, planetary domination, explosions and particles — the systems that make EV Nova EV Nova are in and playable. Call it ~90% of a full port. What's left:

Polish & fidelity

AI, flight feel, spawn cadence and the hundred small behaviors that separate a finished port from one you can't tell apart from 2002.

Bugs, crashes & performance

Edge cases and frame rates, from iPhone to Mac, as more people play on more devices.

Multiplayer polish

Wider multi-device testing, a couple of finer PvP toggles, and smoother authority handoff when a host drops mid-session.

Found something off? The issue tracker is the best place to tell us.

What's coming

Where we're taking it next.

Multiplayer, controllers, Apple TV and iCloud sync are already done and shipping. Here's what's still ahead — none of it disturbs a pure Classic run.

Godot port for Linux & Windows

A second frontend on Godot 4, bridged to the very same Swift engine — no fork, no separate logic. Flight, HUD and landing/launch already run on real game data; the galaxy map, spaceport screens and story runtime are next.

Build & run it yourself

Up and running in a few steps.

You'll need a Mac with Xcode and its command-line tools. Your EV Nova data stays on your machine — git-ignored, never uploaded.

Clone the repo

Grab the source from GitHub.

Fetch dependencies

Run scripts/setup.sh.

Add your own data

Copy your EV Nova files into data/base/ — see docs/GET_THE_DATA.md.

Build & play

Open app/NovaSwift.xcodeproj, pick a target, hit Run.

# 1 · clone git clone https://github.com/SirStig/\ MacOS-iOS-iPadOS-EV-Nova.git cd MacOS-iOS-iPadOS-EV-Nova # 2 · fetch open-source dependencies scripts/setup.sh # 3 · add your EV Nova data into data/base/ # (see docs/GET_THE_DATA.md) # 4 · optional: free community plug-ins scripts/fetch-plugins.sh # 5 · quick check from the command line swift build && swift test # …then open the app in Xcode to play: open app/NovaSwift.xcodeproj

The one rule

You bring the game.

We ship the code; you supply the data. EV Nova's content is owned by ATMOS, so this project contains zero copyrighted game data and never will. NOVA Swift reads a copy you already own, at runtime — the same model as OpenMW and OpenRA.
